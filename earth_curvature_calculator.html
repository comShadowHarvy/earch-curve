Mathematical Insights & Common Misconceptions

The famous formula h ≈ 8" × d² is actually the mathematical Taylor-series parabolic approximation of the Real Spherical Earth!

For a sphere of radius \(R = 3,959\text{ miles}\), the drop from a tangent plane is \(\sqrt{R^2 + d^2} - R \approx \frac{d^2}{2R}\).
\(\frac{1}{2R} = \frac{1}{7,918}\text{ mi/mi}^2 = \frac{5,280 \times 12}{7,918}\text{ in/mi}^2 \approx 7.98\text{ in/mi}^2 \approx 8\text{ in/mi}^2\).

Flat Earthers quote this formula from Samuel Rowbotham’s 19th-century Zetetic Astronomy, ironically using the real sphere’s formula while ignoring observer eye-level height and atmospheric refraction, both of which dramatically reduce the obscured height.

While \(8\text{ in/mi}^2\) approximates the drop from an observer's horizontal tangent line, it fails to calculate hidden height because:

  • It ignores observer eye height: An observer standing 6 ft tall pushes the geometric horizon out by 3 miles, so curvature doesn't begin hiding objects until beyond that point.
  • It ignores downward atmospheric refraction: Normal air density gradients bend light rays downward along the curve of the Earth, extending visibility by ~8% to 15% under standard conditions and even more during temperature inversions.

In springtime, warm air flows over the freezing waters of Lake Michigan, creating an intense temperature inversion where cold, dense air hugs the water surface and warmer air lies aloft.

Because light travels slower in colder, denser air, light rays bend in a downward arc toward the Earth's surface (an optical phenomenon known as looming or superior mirage, with \(k \ge 1.5 - 2.0\)). This ray curvature acts like an atmospheric waveguide, carrying light around the Earth's physical curve and allowing the upper floors of Chicago skyscrapers to be photographed from the Michigan shoreline 50+ miles away.
